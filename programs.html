Programs & Approach

Planned. Not canned.

Some speakers arrive with the same two hours they gave the last audience, take the applause, and catch the flight home. Dave builds each program after he has seen your operation with his own eyes — and he’s still there when the hard questions start.

Where Every Engagement Starts

The Discovery Visit.

A valid action plan requires a real diagnosis. So before Dave recommends anything, he spends two days inside your organization — not in a conference room, in the operation.

Dinner with the president

The first evening belongs to leadership. Dave sits down with the president and the C‑suite and listens to what they believe is happening in their house.

A full day in the operation

The next day he sees everything — the floor, the routes, the back of the house, the places the org chart doesn’t show. Whatever your people do, he watches it done.

Breakfast, and the plan

Before he leaves, he tells leadership what he found and exactly what he would build. If it sounds right, the program moves forward.

It is a commitment on both sides — and it is why the plan he hands you at breakfast is specific enough to act on, whoever ends up carrying it out.

The Programs

Built from what he found — never from a binder.

Guest Astonishment Programs

Three-to-five-hour working sessions — hands-on, tailored to your industry and your people. The C‑suite goes first, because the Internal Guest sets the ceiling for the External Guest. Then the idea moves through every team they lead.

3–5 hours · leadership first

The Mystery Guest

Dave experiences your business the way your guests do — a shopper in the aisle, a diner at the table, even a patient admitted for three days. Then he sits down with the C‑suite and tells them everything he found.

Undercover · C‑suite debrief

Re-Orientation

Orientation happens once — the day someone is hired — and then it fades. Re‑Orientation is the reset: multi-day engagements in small groups that renew the standard for every team member. Clients bring it back yearly, or every two to three years.

Small groups · recurring

DISC Team Profiles

An online assessment ahead of the program; a fifteen-page individual report in every participant’s hands the day of. Dominant, Influential, Stable, Compliant — the ideal team blends all four, and Dave shows yours where it leans. Offered with multi-program packages.

Assessment · individual reports

How a Program Gets Built

The retreat that became a campsite.

A healthcare client called wanting a C‑suite retreat at a grand old resort — ballrooms, white columns, everyone in their own room. Dave asked what the retreat was actually for. Team-building, they said. So he planned them something no ballroom could deliver: three days in Pigeon Forge — Broadway in the Smokies.

A motor coach with a singing cowboy. Executives drawing names from a hat and pitching their own tents with whoever fate assigned them. A cookout that night, breakfast at the campsite, horseback through the mountains, an evening show, a round of golf — and hours of real program threaded through all of it. The tents weren’t a stunt: you can’t stay in your own room when you’re holding the other end of the pole.

Other clients have since asked for that same program. It didn’t come from a catalog — it came from listening.

No ballroom ever built a team like a two-man tent.
